---
name: script-style-kids
description: Writing rules for animated children's series episodes (ages 3–8, ~5 min) — a narrated cartoon with recurring characters who speak their own lines in their own voices; illustrated scenes with camera moves plus many key moments animated by the local video model; no avatar, no real footage.
---
**Format: one episode of a series (~5 min).** The channel is the series. Its brief holds the series bible: "Personagens:" (fixed look and personality of each character), "Mundo:" (where they live), "Temas:" (the kind of lessons). `channel.characters` lists the characters that already have a voice — reuse those exact names. Read `channel_previous_titles` and continue the series: new adventure, same characters, no repeated plot. `channel_previous_episode.ending` is how the last episode ended: when it teased the next adventure, this episode keeps that promise — the opening recalls the teaser in one line and the story is the one announced (adapt the topic you were given to it, never contradict the teaser). Title it "<Série> – Ep. N: <título>", N taken from the project's title (see "Episode number" below).

**The channel brief wins.** When the brief sets its own "Público", "Duração", "Formato" or "Viral" rules (e.g. a family series for kids AND adults, episodes of at most 4 minutes), follow the brief over the defaults below (audience, sentence length, scene count, `target_duration_s`); the safety rules below always apply, except that a series whose brief says it is not for children (e.g. an animated history channel for adults) follows its brief's own content rules: it may portray real historical and public figures (facts with sources) and wars or death symbolically, never graphically.

- Audience: children 3–8 and the parents watching with them. Simple words, short sentences (max ~12 words), warm and playful, gentle humour, onomatopoeia, a recurring catchphrase per character.
- Structure (`target_duration_s` 270–330, 55–65 scenes of ≤ 5 s, ~650–750 words in pt-BR):
  1. Opening (≈20 s): the narrator's fixed series greeting + who we meet today.
  2. Act 1 (≈1 min): ordinary day → a small problem or wish appears.
  3. Act 2 (≈2 min): 2–3 attempts that don't work, each funny or surprising; friends help.
  4. Act 3 (≈1 min): the idea that works, happy solution, celebration.
  5. Closing (≈30 s): the lesson in one sentence, a recap question to the child, a concrete teaser for the next episode (what the next adventure is about, e.g. "Amanhã, a Cacau vai descobrir de onde vem o arco-íris!" — not just "qual será a próxima aventura?"), the fixed goodbye.
- **Characters speak.** About half of the scenes are a character's own line: set `scene.speaker` to the character's name and write in `narration` ONLY what they say, in first person, without "disse a Cacau" (the voice already tells who speaks). Narrator scenes have no `speaker`. One speaker per scene; split a dialogue into consecutive scenes. Write real conversations: characters talk TO each other (questions, answers, jokes, disagreeing then agreeing), at least 6 back-and-forth exchanges of 3+ lines between two or more characters across the episode, not just lines to the camera; the narrator links the exchanges. Use `delivery` for emotion ("excited, giggling", "whispering, curious").
- **Cast voices.** Fill `cast` with every speaking character: name → an English voice description that fits the character and stays recognisable for a child ("small turtle girl, high-pitched, bright and curious, fast and playful"; "round little owl boy, low, slow, warm and sleepy"; "tiny bird girl, very high, quick, chirpy"). Characters already in `channel.characters` keep their voice — repeat their saved description. The narrator uses the channel's voice.
- Safety (non-negotiable): no violence, danger played as fun, scary imagery, villains who hurt, bullying as a joke, food/medicine misuse, unsafe stunts, brands, real people, or calls to buy anything. Nothing romantic or body-focused. No requests for comments, links, "inscreva-se" or personal data (made-for-kids rules). Fears are handled gently and always resolved.
- **Cast looks.** Fill `cast_looks` with every character that appears in any image (speaking or not): name → its fixed look in English, copied from the channel's "Personagens", followed by the series' art style ("a small yellow turtle with big brown eyes, a green shell with one heart-shaped spot and a red cap, 3D animated cartoon, soft Pixar-like style, bright pastel colours"). Say the eye colour and the number of spots, ears or wings when it matters. Same names as in `cast`. The first time a character appears, its reference drawing is made from this text and every later scene and episode follows that drawing, so write it once, carefully, and repeat it unchanged in later episodes.
- **Who is in the picture.** Set `visual.characters` on every scene to the names of the characters visible in it (`[]` for a scene with nobody, e.g. a landscape). Only names from `cast_looks`. At most 4 per scene: with more, split the moment into two scenes.
- Character bible in every image: start every `image_prompt` with the same art style and the full fixed description of each character in the scene, copied word for word from the channel's "Personagens", e.g. "3D animated cartoon, soft Pixar-like style, bright pastel colours, warm light. Cacau, a small yellow turtle with big brown eyes, a green shell with one heart-shaped spot and a red cap, …" then the action and setting. Same setting description whenever a place repeats. No text in images. Never a national flag in an `image_prompt` (AI draws flags wrong): for a flag moment set `visual.flag` to the ISO-2 code (e.g. "AE") and the official flag is shown instead.
- Visuals: the episode is 100% animated. Every scene is `ai_image` with a `motion_prompt` (one clear, gentle action, e.g. "Cacau talks excitedly, her mouth moving, head bobbing, camera still"; "Gil flaps his wings and laughs, leaves sway"; "the stream sparkles, petals float by, slow pan"). Every scene must fit the 5 s animation: narration ≤ 5 s (8–12 words), `target_seconds` ≤ 5 — split longer moments into consecutive scenes (new angle or next beat of the action). Say "camera still" unless a move helps the story. Never `avatar_full`/`avatar_pip`, `broll`, `footage`, `headline` or `source_clip`.
- `voice_style` (narrator): "warm, gentle, smiling storyteller, slow and very clear, playful rises on questions". `on_screen_text`: none, or the lesson at the end.
- `sources`: only when the episode teaches a real fact (cite it); otherwise empty.
- Opening + channel bumper: the first 20–40 s are the episode's opening — start right inside the fun (a character in a funny or curious situation, a surprise, a question the story will answer), never a slow "era uma vez" warm-up first. Put that first line in `hook` and the promise of the adventure in `promise`. End the opening on a small cliffhanger ("…mas o que tinha dentro da caixa?"), and set `intro_end` to that scene's index: the series' short bumper (the logo and a short sound or the series' opening tune, ≤ 3–5 s, no words) plays right after it. Never ask for likes, follows or subscriptions anywhere (no `cta_points` in kids episodes); the ending calls the next episode.
- Chapters: give the acts as `blocks` ({title: a short happy chapter name, scenes: that act's scene indices}) after the opening; scene and block lengths follow this skill, not the 60–90 s / 45 s of the general long-video rule.
- Episode number: it is the one in the project's title ("… – Ep. 13"), never the series' next or latest number (prefs.last_episode / episode_max are the newest episode, not this one). An episode whose project title has no number gets no number in any title.
- Thumbnail (`packaging`): `title` the episode's fun question or surprise; `thumbnail_text` 2–3 big, simple, happy words that don't repeat the title ("CADÊ O BOLO?", "SURPRESA!"; never more than 3); set `thumbnail_scene` to the scene index whose image shows the main characters biggest, CALM and FRIENDLY (smiling, mouths closed or a small smile — never a distorted face, a wide-open or shouting mouth, a scared/angry/crying face or anything scary); that image becomes the background with the words at the top, and the visual judge swaps it for another scene if it breaks this rule; no `thumbnail_prompt`. Two `alternatives` with different words.
- **Cada episódio, uma história diferente.** Before writing, read `channel_previous_titles` and `channel_previous_episode.ending`: never reuse the situation (same problem, same place, same kind of solution) or the ending/lesson of the recent episodes — if the topic you were given repeats one, pick a different angle and say so in the `set_job_state` note.
