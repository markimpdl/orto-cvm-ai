---
name: script-style-lesson
description: Writing rules for video lessons — the presenter teaches from the user's own slides (PDF pages), images and videos, in order, toward the lesson objective written in the topic.
---
- The material is the user's: `user_media` holds the slides (one image per PDF page, description "Slide N/M de <deck>: <page text>"), plus any images and videos. `project.media_mode` is usually "mine": every scene is `user_media` (or a `chart` for numbers), plus the presenter's `avatar_full` scenes below; no stock, footage or AI images.
- The lesson objective comes in the topic ("Objetivo da aula: …" / "Lesson objective: …"). Everything serves it: by the end the viewer can do or explain what the objective says. Teach only what the slides and files support; research is for checking facts and adding one concrete example, never for changing the lesson.
- Opening follows `get_project` → `opening` (off by default on this channel): `hook` false = the lesson starts straight on the content — `hook` is the plain opening line that says what the viewer will learn ("Nesta aula você vai aprender a…" / "In this lesson you'll learn how to…"), no `hook_type`, `hook_variants` or `promise`. `hook` true = a real hook (the problem this lesson solves, or the result they'll get) + a one-line promise, then the lesson. `bumper` true = set `intro_end` to the scene that ends the opening (the channel bumper plays there).
- Recorded parts: a video with `keep_audio: true` (the user teaching on camera or recording his screen) plays with its own sound — `visual.keep_audio: true`, `narration: ""`, `target_seconds` = the part's length; the generated narration only introduces it and continues after it. Everything else is narrated.
- Order: every slide, in deck order (Slide 1, 2, 3…), each at least once — never skip, never reorder slides. A user image or video goes where its description fits, between the slides it belongs to. A dense slide may span 2–3 consecutive scenes (same `media_id`), each explaining one part of it.
- Explain, don't read: say what the slide means, why it matters and one example from real work. Quote at most a short phrase of the slide text. Speak to one learner, warm and clear, like a good instructor ("repara aqui", "look at this part"), short sentences.
- Presenter: the podcast desk (`presenter: "podcast"`): he stays in the small window over the slides the whole time, so slide scenes are plain `user_media`. `avatar_full` for the opening, the recap and 1–3 moments that need a face (a key warning, a personal tip), ~15–20% of the time.
- Timing: the slide stays on screen while it is explained — `target_seconds` from the narration (~2.5 words per second), 10–45 s per scene (split longer explanations over more scenes on the same slide).
- Blocks: one `block` per section of the deck (a title slide or a change of subject starts a new one); the block title = the section in a few words (YouTube chapters). Block `question` = what this section answers; `reveal` = the takeaway. No cliffhanger continuity lines; a simple bridge ("agora que você entendeu X, vamos para Y").
- Subscribe ask (`cta_points`): short and calm, "mid" after a section ends near 40%, and "end". Never at the start.
- Ending: a 3-point recap tied to the objective (`avatar_full`), one practice task or question for the comments (`ending.comment_question`), and the next lesson (`ending.next_video`).
- Packaging: title = the skill the viewer gains, clear and searchable ("Como fazer X em 10 minutos"); `thumbnail_text` = the outcome in 2–3 words (benefit or result, not a neutral label), honest.
- Slides past the preview images (only the first 16 come as pictures) are known by their description text: trust it, and don't describe visuals you didn't see.
